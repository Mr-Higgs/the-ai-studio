# Grand Slam Offer Playbook (paraphrased from *$100M Offers*, Alex Hormozi)

A Grand Slam Offer = an attractive promotion + an unmatchable value proposition + a premium price + an unbeatable guarantee. It makes the business **incomparable** to commodity competitors, so prospects judge it on value, not price.

---

## 1. Market First: The Starving Crowd

Order of importance: **Market > Offer strength > Persuasion skill.** A great offer in a bad market still fails.

A good market has all four:
1. **Massive pain** - they *need* it, not just want it. Pain sets your price ceiling.
2. **Purchasing power** - they can actually pay a premium.
3. **Easy to target** - you can reach them (associations, lists, platforms, local areas).
4. **Growing** - the market is expanding, not shrinking.

Core evergreen markets: **Health, Wealth, Relationships.** Niche inside one of them.
- Niching down lets you charge far more for the same product (specific beats general).
- Commit to a niche; don't hop markets after one failed offer - fix the offer instead.

## 2. Pricing Principles

- Charge premium prices: higher price -> more margin -> more to spend on getting results and acquiring customers -> better clients who value it more.
- Goal: get more people to say yes **at a higher price** by raising perceived value, not by cutting price.
- Never discount the core offer to close; **add bonuses** instead.

## 3. The Value Equation

```
            Dream Outcome  x  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value  =  -------------------------------------------------------
            Perceived Time Delay  x  Perceived Effort & Sacrifice
```

| Driver | Goal | Prospect's question | Levers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dream Outcome | Increase | "What will I get?" | Status-raising, specific, vivid result |
| Perceived Likelihood | Increase | "Will it actually work for *me*?" | Proof, testimonials, specificity, guarantees, credentials |
| Time Delay | Decrease | "How long until I see it?" | Fast first win, quick onboarding, early milestones |
| Effort & Sacrifice | Decrease | "What do I have to do/give up?" | DFY, templates, tools, convenience |

- Big companies win on the bottom (time and effort). Anyone can promise an outcome; making it fast and effortless is hard and valuable.
- What matters is the **perception**: the prospect must *see* the higher likelihood and the lower time and effort.
- Sell the vacation, not the plane flight.

## 4. Building the Offer (5 Steps)

### Step 1 - Dream Outcome
Avatar + specific result + timeframe. e.g. "Lose 20 lbs in 6 weeks."

### Step 2 - List Problems
Go through every step the customer must take, in order (before, during, after). For each step, list every reason they might fail, in the four buckets:
- **Outcome**: "It won't be worth it financially."
- **Likelihood**: "It won't work for me / I won't stick with it / life will get in the way." (most service-specific)
- **Effort**: "Too hard, confusing, I won't like it, I'll be bad at it."
- **Time**: "Takes too long, I'm too busy, not convenient."

More problems = more opportunities for value. Aim for dozens.

### Step 3 - Solutions List
Flip each problem into a solution, then name it: "How to [X] without [Y] even if [Z]." Every unsolved objection is one more reason someone doesn't buy - solve them all.

### Step 4 - Delivery Vehicles ("The How") - Cheat Codes
1. **Personal attention**: 1-on-1, small group, one-to-many.
2. **Effort expected from them**: DIY (they figure it out), DWY (you teach them), DFY (you do it).
3. **Live medium**: in-person, phone, email, text, Zoom, chat.
4. **Recorded format**: audio, video, written.
5. **Response speed and availability**: 24/7, 9-5, within 5 min, 1 hr, 24 hrs.
6. **10x / 1/10th test**: What would you deliver if they paid 10x? How would you still get them results at 1/10th the price?

Think divergently first - list anything, even things you wouldn't actually do.

### Step 5 - Trim & Stack
- Remove **high-cost, low-value** first, then **low-cost, low-value**.
- Keep **low-cost/high-value** and **high-cost/high-value**.
- Test each item: will they value it financially? Does it raise belief they'll succeed? Does it cut effort? Does it cut time?
- If an ideal item is too expensive to deliver, step back to a lesser version you can deliver at scale (or raise the price).
- Favour **one-to-many assets** (templates, tools, calculators, recorded trainings): high one-off creation cost, near-zero marginal cost, value forever.
- For your first offer, over-deliver like crazy, then systemise to lower cost.
- Bundle the survivors into the final high-value deliverable.

## 5. Enhancing the Offer

### Scarcity (limits on quantity) - must be honest
1. **Limited seats/slots** - total, or per period (e.g. "5 new clients per month").
2. **Limited bonuses** - e.g. first 10 buyers get X.
3. **Never available again** - one-time release/edition.
- Honest scarcity: publicly share real capacity ("81% full", "2 spots left").
- Always sell out, then *announce* that you sold out (social proof for next time).

### Urgency (limits on time) - must be real
1. **Rolling cohorts** - fixed start dates (weekly, monthly). "Sign up today to start Monday, otherwise wait for the next group."
2. **Rolling seasonal promos** - the same offer re-wrapped by season/holiday with a real end date.
3. **Pricing/bonus-based** - the promo, discount, or bonus expires, not the service. Announce real price rises ahead of time.
4. **Exploding opportunity** - the opportunity itself decays over time (arbitrage, early-mover advantage).
- Most sales come right at the deadline. Deadlines drive decisions.

### Bonuses
- A single offer is worth less than the same thing split into parts and stacked as bonuses.
- Anchor price to the core offer, then grow the price-to-value gap with each bonus.
- 1-on-1 selling: ask for the sale first; if yes, reveal bonuses as a "wow". If no, add a bonus that kills their specific objection, then ask again.
- Bonus rules:
  1. Always offer them.
  2. Give each a special name with a benefit in the title.
  3. Explain how it relates to their issue, what it is, how you discovered/built it, and how it makes things faster or easier.
  4. Add proof that it's valuable.
  5. Paint a picture of life after using it.
  6. Always give it a price tag and justify it.
  7. Tools and checklists beat more training (less effort and time).
  8. Each should crush a specific "why I can't succeed" belief.
  9. Solve the *next* problem before they hit it.
  10. Total bonus value should eclipse the core offer.
  11. Add scarcity/urgency to the bonuses themselves.
- **Advanced**: partner bonuses - non-competing businesses contribute products/services in exchange for exposure to your customers.

### Guarantees (risk reversal = #1 conversion lever)
Formula: **"If you don't get [X result] in [Y time], we will [Z]."** No "or what" = weak guarantee.

Types:
1. **Unconditional** - pay, try, refund if unhappy (strongest, most refunds). Variants: full refund, partial refund, refund + extra costs, pay for a competitor, refund + bonus cash.
2. **Conditional** - "better than money back," tied to key actions the client must take (which also drive results). Variants: outsized refund (2-3x), service guarantee (keep working free until X), modified service (extra time free), credit-based, personal service (you personally step in), hotel/airfare perks, wage-payment, release of service (cancel anytime), delayed second payment (pay rest only after first result), first outcome guarantee (cover costs until first result).
3. **Anti-guarantee** - "All sales final," with a compelling reason why (you expose IP, the product is consumable, you reserve real resources).
4. **Implied** - performance-based: rev-share, profit-share, pay on results, triggers/ratchets. Needs measurable outcomes and trust/control over payment.
- **Stack guarantees**: e.g. unconditional 30-day + conditional 90-day "double your money back"; or sequential milestones.
- **Name your guarantee** something vivid and memorable (not "30-Day Money Back Guarantee").
- Build it by reversing their biggest fears: "What are they afraid will happen if they pay you?"
- Guarantees enhance a good offer; they can't save a bad product or sales team.
- Starting out: service-based guarantees or performance partnerships keep sales final and keep you honest.

## 6. Naming: The MAGIC Formula

Use 3-5 components, in whatever order sounds punchiest. Short beats long.

| Letter | Component | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M** | Make a **Magnetic** reason why | Why this offer now / what's in it for me | Free, 88% Off, Giveaway, Grand Opening, New Year, Back to School, Anniversary, Halloween, Summer |
| **A** | **Announce your Avatar** | Call out who it's for (hyper-local if local) | Towson Moms, Salon Owners, Brick & Mortar Businesses, Busy Execs |
| **G** | **Give them a Goal** | The dream outcome, specific and tangible | Pain Free, Celebrity Smile, Double Your Profit, First Client, 7 Figure, Little Black Dress |
| **I** | **Indicate a time Interval** | How long it takes | 4-Hour, 21-Day, 6-Week, 90-Day, 12-Month |
| **C** | **Complete with a Container word** | Signals a bundled system, not a commodity | Challenge, Blueprint, Bootcamp, Intensive, Accelerator, Fast Track, Sprint, Reset, Masterclass, Mastermind, Incubator, Detox, Transformation, Game Plan, Deep Dive, Workshop, Shortcut, Liftoff, Cheatcode, System, Experience |

- Compliance: don't pair a quantified claim (income, weight) with a duration on ad platforms that ban it. Use a non-claim goal instead ("Make Your First Sale in 10 Days").
- Pro tips: **rhyme** (Six-Pack Fast Track, 12-Month No-Debt Reset) or **alliterate** (Debt Detox, Real Estate Reset). Don't force either.
- **Name every sub-item and bonus** with MAGIC too - it raises perceived value.
- Example shapes: "Free 6-Week Lean-By-Halloween Challenge", "5 Clients in 5 Days Blueprint", "Back Sore No More! 90-Day Rapid Healing Intensive".

## 7. Offer Fatigue & Rotation

Same offer, new wrapper. When results drop, change things in this order (lightest to heaviest):
1. Creative (images/video)
2. Ad body copy
3. Headline / wrapper (name, seasonal theme)
4. Duration
5. Enhancer (the free/discount/bonus component)
6. Monetisation structure / price points

Test 2-3 names, keep the winner as the control, and keep testing new names against it. Once an offer makes money, rarely change the machine behind it.
